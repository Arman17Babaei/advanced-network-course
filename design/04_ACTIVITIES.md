# 4. Activities

**Course/Module/Session:** Advanced Networks  
**Date:** Fall 2026

This section contains learning experiences intended to help students reach the outcomes in [2. Learning Outcomes](02_LEARNING_OUTCOMES.md). Activities do not need to produce a submitted artifact or a grade. Evidence used to decide whether an outcome has actually been achieved belongs in [3. Assessments](03_ASSESSMENTS.md).

Now it’s time to think about how pre-work, independent work, project work, and in-person class time support learning.

- **Aligned Learning Outcome(s):** Copy/paste a learning outcome from [2. Learning Outcomes](02_LEARNING_OUTCOMES.md). The activity should help students build toward that outcome.
- **Prework:** Identify the pre-class or independent activities and resources through which students gain the knowledge needed to participate effectively.
- **Activities:** Plan the learning experiences through which students engage with the outcome.
- **AI Use:** Decide whether to require, allow, or ban AI use for the learning activity. AI use in activities does not need to match the AI policy of the later assessment.

## Practice assignments

Practice assignments are examples of the level and type of work students are expected to learn to do. They are **not submitted for credit**.

Their purpose is to let students practice foundations, mechanisms, performance reasoning, architecture analysis, and other course skills before individual quizzes and exams. Students may use AI while learning from these assignments unless a particular activity states otherwise.

The course should avoid interpreting completion of practice work as evidence of learning. Evidence comes from the individual assessments.

## AI-assisted paper study

Paper analysis is used twice in the activity sequence, but the two instances have different roles.

The first analysis is explicitly scaffolded: students use a guidance sheet before class, then the class discusses and performs the analysis together. The paper should connect either to foundations already covered or to network design.

For the second analysis, students are given only the paper name/reference and use the same guidance framework independently. There is no instructor-led analysis or in-class discussion before assessment. The paper may be shared by the whole class or selected per project, and should ideally contribute concepts, mechanisms, architecture ideas, methodology, or evidence useful for later project work.

The guidance should direct students to questions such as:

- What problem is the paper solving?
- What assumptions make the proposed design reasonable?
- How is the architecture decomposed?
- How do the mechanisms follow from the assumptions?
- What alternatives were possible?
- What does the evaluation actually establish?
- What evidence would fail to support the authors’ conclusion?
- Which ideas might transfer to the project?

Students may use AI while preparing both analyses. The second analysis is followed directly by the individual no-AI research-analysis quiz, with no intervening in-class paper discussion.

## Project presentations as a learning activity for the audience

For students who are not presenting, the final project presentations are part of the course’s learning material.

Students should use the presentations to build:

- awareness of contemporary technologies and where they are used;
- understanding of unfamiliar mechanisms and systems;
- a library of concrete architecture cases;
- intuition about how workloads and constraints change design choices;
- examples of experiments, failures, discoveries, and redesign.

These presentations may later be used as source material for architecture analysis and design questions in the final exam, so students should focus on transferable principles rather than implementation trivia.
